Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about Lot Current: whether it is allowed on Facebook, what it reads, where your data is, and why you click Publish, never the tool.

Questions and answers

Is this allowed on Facebook?

Here is the honest answer. Meta's Terms prohibit accessing its products by automated means without permission. Lot Current fills in the form and opens pages; a person checks every listing and clicks Publish, and nothing is posted or edited in the background or while you're away. That is the safest design available. It is not a guarantee, and no tool can honestly promise one. Facebook's rules apply to you when you post. If Facebook ever warns you about your listings, stop and tell your manager.

Does it post for me?

No. Lot Current never clicks Publish, Update, Delete or Mark as sold. It fills in the Marketplace form and opens the right page; you check it and click Publish yourself, every time. There is no code for clicking those buttons, and a test that fails if any appears.

What does it read?

Your dealership's public website: the used inventory, the store's name and address, and one car's full record at post time. On the Marketplace create-listing page it reads back the fields it filled so it can tell you what the form shows, and on one of your own listings that you open from the To do tab to update or take down, it reads that listing's title, price and sold status to see when your change shows. It does not read your Facebook messages, cookies or password, and it does not track your browsing anywhere else.

Where is my data?

In your own browser, in Chrome's storage. Your scans, settings and list of posts are kept on your computer. Your profile (your name, role and closing line, the dealership's name, town, state and ZIP, its stores, price basis, price note and daily cap, your listing defaults, the rewrite-service address and whether it is on, your Terms acceptance, and the website it was saved on and when) also follows your Chrome sign-in: Chrome keeps it in its sync storage under your own Google account. Forget my synced profile in Settings removes it from there; Clear everything for this website does not. Nothing from Facebook is kept beyond the links to your own listings (the ones you save, or that Lot Current records after you click Publish) and, only while a post or an update is under way, what the form or the listing showed. A VIN goes to NHTSA only when you click Check with NHTSA. With the rewrite service turned on in Settings, the car's facts (not its VIN or price) and its website description, the dealership's name and city, your name and role and the price note go to that service and on to Anthropic's API, which drafts the description; to guess a colour the website does not give, up to four of the car's photo addresses go the same way. When your dealership uses Lot Current accounts (not open yet), your posted list, post timings, to-do items and scan counts also sync to its records, where everyone signed in at your dealership sees your posted list, to-do items and scan counts, and only you and your managers see your post timings; the privacy policy says what is kept and for how long.

What about my Facebook password?

Lot Current never asks for it, never reads your cookies or tokens, and never runs extra or shared accounts. You sign in to Facebook yourself, in your own browser, and Lot Current never sees that.

Which websites work?

Dealership websites on Dealer Inspire, which is where Lot Current has been checked on a live site. It also has readers for DealerOn and Dealer.com websites, and tries any website that publishes standard vehicle data for search engines; those readers have been tested only on sample websites so far, so no other platform is known to work on a real site yet. Other website platforms are added one at a time, based on demand. Ask us about yours.

How do updates arrive?

Today Lot Current installs as a file you unzip into a folder and load in Chrome; an update is a new file you unzip over the same folder, and your settings and listings stay put. A Chrome Web Store listing is planned, after which Chrome installs updates the way it does for any extension.
