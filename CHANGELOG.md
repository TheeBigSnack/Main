- **DealerOn and Dealer.com readers** (`extension/adapters/dealerOn.js`, `dealerCom.js`, on the shared `inventoryJson.js`; before the standard-data reader in `ADAPTERS`). The 2026-10-01 page-text survey (server HTML through a web-fetch tool; notes in the project's shared files) found three local dealer groups on DealerOn and one on Dealer.com, and on both platforms the used list page draws its cars with scripts, so the standard-data reader would have found no car links. Each reader takes the inventory-list address the page's own script asked for when it loaded (from the browser's resource timing list: no address, dealer id or key is guessed), reads it page by page from the dealer tab (DealerOn's `pt` page number, Dealer.com's `start` offset; a page that repeats the first ends the scan as not complete rather than reading wrong), and from the service worker for rescans, on the website's own origin only, so **no new permission**. Field names are read tolerantly (with or without DealerOn's `Vehicle` prefix, or in attribute lists; nested objects only for the store name), the cars come from the answer's largest list (never a featured list beside it), and a field it can't name stays empty, which sends the car to Needs a look or Not ready as always. **Prices:** every labelled price on the record is read; the price is the one the platform marks final or labels as the selling or dealer's own price ("Internet Price", or "<Dealer> Price" when its words are in the dealership name the record carries), with the fee included as those sites show it, and the base price below it ("Retail", plain "Price") becomes the lower second price, so the dealer's existing price-basis setting chooses between them and the wizard's fee suggestion reads the gap (no dealer value in code). MSRP, previous, payments, fees, rebates, conditional, employee, wholesale, book, KBB and market figures are never the price; two selling prices that disagree, or a named price the dealership's name doesn't explain, leave the car with no price ("Two prices on the website") for a person to look at. A first answer with no cars that doesn't say the lot is empty stops the scan. **Sold cars:** a car missing from the list is gone only when its own page answers 404 or 410 without a redirect (DealerOn 404, Dealer.com 410, as the survey reported); a page still showing the car, a redirect or an unknown page leaves it "missing but not confirmed gone"; a server error or refusal marks nothing gone. At most 12 missing cars are checked per scan, and on DealerOn those page reads keep the fixed 10-second gap its robots.txt asks for. **At post time** the car's own page adds every photo (DealerOn's `/inventoryphotos/<id>/<vin>/ip/<n>.jpg`, thumbnails and other cars' photos left out; on Dealer.com only the page's standard data for this VIN, never its similar-car photos) and its description. Posting from a car's own page works once the website has been scanned (`src/vehicleDetails.js` fills the list address from the last scan's stored service of the same adapter); before that, it says to open the used inventory page and wait until the cars show. Tests: `test/platformAdapters.test.js` and the adapter contract over synthetic sites (`test/platformSites.js`), and the new e2e flow `test/e2e/platforms.e2e.mjs` (`npm run test:e2e:platforms`, in CI) on two mock websites whose list pages draw their cards from JSON: scan, post one DealerOn car with all four photos from its page (the test clicks Publish), then a day-2 rescan from the worker that finds the sold car (404 / 410) and the price drop. **Not yet checked on a real DealerOn or Dealer.com website**: this container cannot reach dealer sites, and the field names and paging parameters come from public sources (Dealer.com's Web Integration API documentation, a public write-up of DealerOn's list data) and the survey; `extension/adapters/README.md` lists what one `npm run survey` run must confirm.
